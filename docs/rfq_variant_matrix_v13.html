Review of the v1.3 run (2026-10-29): summary, findings, thoughts

Summary

Seventy-one minutes, 30 settings, replay exact, every cache rebuilt because the frontier range changed (the next run reuses all of it). Three results. First, the stacks are additive without the manual charge and not with it: in pair C the curve plus the width charge is worth more than either alone, at the charge's P&L and the curve's cover saving; in pair A the width charge alone is still the best row and adding a curve buys dollars with cover and risk. Second, the width charge on every request over-widens as scaled: the fitted widths of the uncovered 70% are far larger than the covered ones, so the same gamma turns a selective charge into a blanket one and the win rate falls to 0.7%. Third, month by month the pair C edge decays from May to August and turns negative in September, the month the manual charge jumped, then recovers in October.

The stacks

  • Pair C, additive. The best stack is worth +0.28 bp per request against +0.20 for the charge alone and +0.17 for the curve alone, with the curve's cover saving (−1.9 bp) and the charge's dollars (+1.7 million a month) in the same row, t 3.7 on the common markout and 3.3 hedged. Levelled to production's own win rate it reads +0.45 bp and 2.5 million a month. The two terms act on different things, the curve on the price of the kept wins and the charge on which wins are kept, and the optimizer combines them without either losing its effect. Eighteen of 30 rows pass pair C on cover, all four valuations and dollars; 26 of 30 in May to August.
  • Pair A, not additive. With the manual charge in, the width charge alone (k=2, +0.034) is still the top row, and the three stacks sit below it: the trailing-vol tilt changes nothing (it covers 23% of requests and its rows are the charge's rows to the third decimal), logit 1.5 turns the cover saving into a cover cost (+0.06 to +0.12 bp) and raises book sd by a third for 15% more dollars, stretch 1 gives up 1 bp of cover and 40% of production's wins for 218 thousand a month. The v1.2 reading holds: the traders already price the volatility the curve sees, so on top of their charge the curve has no cover to save, and what it adds is a volume and risk trade.
  • k=1 against k=2. With the manual charge the two are equal on the minimum sceptical delta (+0.034 both) and k=1 keeps 76% of wins against 69% with less risk (sd +15% against +19%), but it pays 0.13 bp more cover. In May to August k=1 is the top pair A row (+0.045, t 4.3). k=1 is the gentler candidate if churn matters more than cover.

The width charge on every request

The width model (least squares of log width on key group, size and tenor buckets, manual class and log idiosyncratic vol, walk-forward) reaches 98% of requests but explains little of the width after June (R2 0.07 to 0.23; 0.50 in June on one training month). The real problem is the scale: gamma is k per bp at the median width of the covered requests, and the covered requests are the active bonds. The uncovered 70% are the quiet ones with wider fitted bands, so the same gamma charges them several bp and the quote goes 6 bp wider on average: win rate 0.7% against production's 3.6% at k=2, 1.2% at k=1. At equal win rate those rows keep 80 to 90% of the comparator's wins and show a tiny positive delta (+0.003 to +0.008 bp, 10 to 22 thousand a month), which is why they appear in the pass list; raw against production they lose 0.06 bp and 115 thousand a month. In pair B they are the worst rows of the run in May to August (−0.22 to −0.35 bp, t −6 to −8). Not a candidate as scaled. The fix is in the scaling, not the idea: gamma set on the extended population's own median, or a separate gamma for the uncovered requests picked walk-forward, or the fitted widths capped at the covered distribution.

Month by month

  • Pair A. The width charge is positive in four months of six (May +0.05, June +0.06, August +0.03, October +0.01) and negative in July (−0.01) and September (−0.03); dollars are positive every month, 33 to 200 thousand. Its cover saving is a June effect (−0.55 bp) with small costs in July, September and October. Logit 1.5 alone is negative every month from May to August and positive, barely, in September and October; the stacks follow the charge in May to August and the curve after. Nothing in pair A is positive in all six months.
  • Pair C. Every candidate is positive in five months and negative in September, and the stacks' delta falls month by month from +0.44 (May) to +0.13 (August) before the September dip and the October recovery (+0.25). September is also where the record-exit dollars are largest, 6.8 to 7.3 million for the charge rows, at a 40% win rate without the manual charge: the record exit and the sceptical valuations disagree on that month, and the dollars column should not be read there. The decay from May to August coincides with the quantile coverage growing from 22% to 31% and with more calibration months, and it needs an explanation before November.

Pair B and the rest

  • Pair B stays closed pooled (0 of 30). The stacks top the table at +0.29 bp but with 6 bp more cover, 46% of wins kept and a comparator pushed 4 bp past where production quoted, t about 1. In September and October stretch 1 and logit 1.5 alone pass pair B as in v1.2.1; the stacks' 9 to 11 million a month there sit on a 10 bp comparator shift and should be discounted.
  • Downside penalty (lambda 1) is again a September and October row: top of pair C there (+0.41, cover +1.9) and top of pair A there (+0.035, cover +2.3), nowhere pooled. Scored on the 28% subset.
  • Concentration k=1 is negative on P&L in every pair and better on risk only. The curve validation is unchanged (logit 0.5 the best held-out log-likelihood, logit 1.5 the best replay). b(x) and the premium are tables and off, as pruned.

Candidates for November

With the manual charge: the quantile width charge k=2 (ls), unchanged, with k=1 as the lower-churn alternative. Without it: logit 1.5 + width k=2 (ls) or stretch 1 + width k=2 (grid), the two stacks, which are the first rows to carry both a cover saving and the P&L at once. The trailing-vol tilt is dropped from the stacks (no effect) and kept as a single term. The extended charge goes back to design.

Next design

Rescale the extended width charge and quote it again; stack k=1 with the manual charge; decompose the pair C stack's cover saving and its month-by-month decay by key group and by coverage class; and read September on its own, with the comparator shift and the valuations side by side.
