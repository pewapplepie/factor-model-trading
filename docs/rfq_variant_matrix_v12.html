Review of the v1.2.1 run (2026-10-28): summary, findings, thoughts

Summary

Ninety minutes, 47 settings, replay exact, the wider frontier matched within 0.5 pp everywhere. Two families win, and they win differently. The idiosyncratic-vol fill curve wins on cover. The quantile-grid width charge wins on P&L and dollars, and it is the first variant to beat production in pair A with the manual charge in. Pair B stays closed pooled. The b(x) double-count fix turned that family from a quote input into an adverse-selection measurement.

What each winner does

  • Quantile width charge. The bond gets a charge proportional to the calibrated half-width of its next-day residual band, so the optimizer quotes wider on bonds whose idiosyncratic move is dispersed and keeps quoting the tight ones. It changes who you win, not the price on the ones you keep: cover barely moves, P&L and dollars jump. The cost is churn (22 to 31% of production's wins given up) and in pair A a bigger book (sd up 19%). It acts on the 28% of requests that have conditional quantiles and equals production elsewhere, so the per-request effect inside that slice is about three times what the table shows.
  • Vol curve. Reprices along the bond's volatility, so the kept wins pay less against the cover. The trailing-sd version is the robust one: 99% kept, common-markout t of 10, no risk change, a third of the bp. Logit 1.5 on the IPCA residual variance has the cover saving.

Additive to the manual charge (pair A)

Yes, for the first time. The width charge is additive in the literal sense, the trader's charge plus a second charge from the bond's residual band. Raw, it wins 2.4% against production's 3.6%. At equal win rate its choice of which wins to drop is worth 0.03 to 0.05 bp per request under every valuation, about 120 thousand dollars a month trimmed; levelled to production's own win rate it is worth 0.06 bp and 258 thousand a month but pays 1.7 bp more against the cover. The trailing-vol tilt keeps 98% of wins and adds 0.005 to 0.009 bp with t 3.3, consistent across both periods. The cover saving of the curves does not survive the manual charge: the trader's charge still does the cover work.

By period

May to August (manual charge about 1 bp) is the pooled picture. September and October (charge 11 to 13 bp, production winning 2%) change three things: the curves start to win in pair B as well, stretch 1 and logit 1.5 beating production with its charge on every axis; the quantile downside penalty becomes the top pair C row at +0.41 bp but gives up 1.9 bp of cover; and the premium and quantile rows in pair B show 7 to 9 million dollars a month. Treat those as the weakest numbers in the run: the comparator is production pushed 12.5 bp past where it ever quoted, with a 1.2 pp win-rate mismatch.

b(x) after the fix

With the double count removed, the record exit says the move from the quoted mid to the exit gets worse the cheaper the winning bid: −1.5 bp to −65 bp across the concession quintiles in the short-call 5% group. That is the winner's curse on the quiet side: when a cheap bid wins, nobody else wanted the bond. The realised round trips say the opposite for the same group (+36 bp on the cheapest quintile) because the desk sold those at a markup. Quoted as a value term, b(x) record is no longer in any top table and b(x) realised stays negative. Closed as a quote input; kept as the clearest adverse-selection measurement the lab has produced. The notebook's bin label read the x axis backwards (fixed in commit 2c3746c; numbers unchanged).

Other families

  • Premium. Calibrated slope 1.9 to 3.3, so the stated factor premium is realised at two to three times its size, yet quoting it pooled adds nothing in pairs A and C. The information is in the extreme months only.
  • Call structure. The refit does nothing. Kappa by call group is a middling curve. The call-aware V5 residual sd is indistinguishable from V0 on the same window, so Section 9's model adds nothing to the quote that the trailing sd does not already carry.
  • Concentration terms. Unchanged as a risk tool. The walk-forward k picks k=1 in four of six months under the dollar criterion, so the edge-of-grid problem is gone.

Caveats

  • Coverage drives the quantile rows: 28% of requests, and the downside rows are scored on that subset alone (their win-rate base reads 25.8% rather than 23.2%).
  • The pair A winner raises book sd by a fifth. On hedged dollars per unit of sd it is still ahead, but that is a risk-budget decision, not a quote decision.
  • Curve validation and replay still disagree: logit 0.5 has the best held-out log-likelihood, logit 1.5 the best replay.
  • On this data the results confirm under rules written after the v1 run; the pre-registered test is on data from November on.

Next design

Stack the width charge on the logit 1.5 curve (the machinery already supports a charge plus a shift), extend the quantile models to the full request set so the charge is not a 28% feature, and judge pair A on hedged dollars per unit of sd against the concentration term. Frozen candidates for November: logit 1.5 with the manual charge, the quantile width charge k=2 with the manual charge, and the stack of the two.
