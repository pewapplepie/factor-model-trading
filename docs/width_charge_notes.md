# The width charge notebook (width charge v1.0.3)

`muni_width_charge_research.py` is one notebook from the data to the backtest for the quoting change the variant programme (`muni_rfq_factor_variants`, v1.1 to v1.3) found worth putting live: a per-bond risk charge sized to the dispersion of the bond's own residual move, the "quantile width charge". It is written to be read by people who have not followed the lab and variant notebooks: every section says what it builds, why, and which plot to look at. It is self-contained: it reads the data pipeline directly and needs neither the lab's cache nor its handoff.

## v1.0.3: the crossing grid (2026-11-03)

The v1.0.2 run (data to October 30, the October 28 pfill in the schedule from the 29th) said the two-sided charge tightens 0.3 bp against a 17.6 bp median gap, so it pays cover on kept wins for a few hundred new wins, and that the street's winning trades richen most on wide bands. v1.0.3 unties what the two-sided charge tied together and sizes the credit from the data. Pre-registered as `width_charge_v103`; 25 settings in one cached stage (Section 8 [4]), scored like every other setting.

- **Asymmetric**: credit `k_t / median W` per bp of band above the trailing median, charge `k_w / median W` per bp below it (credit on wide bands); `k_w` in {1, 2}, `k_t` in {2, 4, 8, 16}. Eight settings.
- **Centre as a quantile**: the reversed two-sided charge at k = 2 with the centre at the trailing 65th and 80th percentile of `W`. Two settings.
- **Expected-markout credit**: `h` times the mean other-side markout of the street's winning trade on the requests we lost, by `W` quintile x key group, fitted on the earlier test months with the quintile edges from those months, the quintile mean where a cell has fewer than `min_fit_rows`, zero where the expectation is not positive; the first test month has no history and gets no credit. `h` in {0.25, 0.5, 1}. Three settings.
- **Expected-margin charge**: `f` times the margin over the cover, a walk-forward least squares of the bp production left on the table on its cover wins (clipped at the training 99th percentile) on the width model's design (key group, size and tenor buckets, manual class, trailing residual sd) plus the log next-day half-width and its presence flag; applied to every winnable request of the month. `f` in {0.25, 0.5, 0.75}. Three settings, and the nine combinations with the markout credit.
- **Select and confirm (Section 10 [3])**: every setting scored against production as is on the first `select_months` test months and on the rest; a setting whose cover saving or win-rate change changes sign between the two windows is noise. The scatter of select against confirm is the picture.

Runtime on the desk: about 16 minutes to quote the 25 settings, an hour to score them, ten minutes for the split and the terms; everything before Section 8 [4] reuses its cache.

## v1.0.2: the win rule in price space, the settle date and the carry charge, crossing the print (2026-11-02)

The first valid run (v1.0.1, June to October) read the candidate as safe and roughly neutral. v1.0.2 consolidates the draft that followed it (the settle date, the price and lost-request checks, a two-sided charge, the scorecard by outcome term) with what that draft's run showed, and adds what the desk asked for: a win rule read in price space through the REST pricing service, the carry finance charge after the optimizer, and the ability to win requests production lost. Pre-registered as `width_charge_v102` in Section 1. The data run to 2026-10-30 (`end_date`). The carry finance charge is on `strategy_output` already (`carryfinancecharges`, logged with the quote, `carryadjustedyield` beside it; the RFQ log is the fallback); the settle date needs the RFQ log re-pulled, every month, since the pull takes every column of `muni_algo_trade_track` and a column missing from the first partition is dropped on read. The notebook resolves both names on the stores and says what it found. `data_pipeline_regen_oct30.ipynb` (percent-format `.py` beside it) is the regeneration notebook for the hub: the plan, the forced re-pull of the RFQ log over the whole window, the forced re-pull of October for every other source, the build, and the checks.

- **The settle date (Section 5 [2]).** Production's logged settle date is sent to the pricing service where it lies between the trade date and T+2 on the bond calendar; T+2 otherwise. The rows outside the window (not logged, before the trade date, beyond T+2) are counted by source, never dropped. The weekday lag as sent is reported.
- **The carry finance charge (Section 5 [1]).** Production loads it after the optimizer, so it sits inside the logged loading `adj_yield = final yield - optimal yield`, which every requote already carries. The check reads the logged carry in yield or bp (whichever matches the loading on more requests), reports the share of requests where the loading equals the carry, and what else is in the loading. Nothing is added a second time: a requote is `signal + x*_new / 100 + adj_yield`, as before.
- **The win rule in price space (Section 5 [2]).** The cover and the matched print are logged as prices; production's quote as a yield and a price. The threshold a quote must beat is `our logged yield + (our logged price - the other price) / DV01`, with DV01 from the service at our logged quote and settle (one call per bond and settle date, at the bond's median logged yield that day). The level comes from production's own (yield, price) pair, so the service's convention gap against production (78% of wins within 0.1 bp, 350 bp tails on callables in the draft run) cancels; only the slope comes from the service, used in production's direction (yield to price) as production uses it. The tape yield of the print is the fallback where a price or DV01 is missing; the gap between the two readings is reported by call bucket, as is the share of lost requests where the two readings disagree on whether the print was richer. The vanilla pass (every logged win and loss reproduced) must still hold. The P&L of a new win is `100 (y_quote - y_print_px) + M`: the price term against the print in our quote's convention (`y_trade_px`), the markout `M` from the tape's own yields as before.
- **The linearisation (Section 7 [2]).** The price-anchored threshold moves along the DV01 tangent at our quote. On a fixed sample (`px_check_sample`, seeded) the service prices the bond at the print's anchored yield and at the cover's, and the result is compared with the logged price: the tangent error at exactly the crossing distance, by the size of the move and by call structure. Second order for bullets (a 30 bp move on a 30-year bond is about 0.01 bp); a yield-to-worst switch on a callable would show here.
- **The price and lost-request checks (Section 7 [2]).** From the draft: the service's price at our logged yield against the logged bid price and the WFTrade fill (logged = WFTrade on 99.8% of wins), by call structure and settle source; the matched print of a lost request checked as the winning bid (found on the tape, same par, print type, minutes after the request, bp behind).
- **The two-sided variations (Section 8 [3]).** `charge_i = gamma (W_i - centre)`, the same `gamma = k / median W`, the centre the trailing median `W` over the previous 20 request days strictly before the request date, `k` in {1, 2}, manual charge kept. Two signs: *two-sided* tightens on bands narrower than the centre (the draft; it lost 0.006 and 0.013 bp on the draft run and crossed 419 and 879 of 1.24 million lost requests, tightening 0.1 to 0.2 bp against a 16 bp median gap); *two-sided, reversed* tightens on the wider bands, the direction the draft's street-markout table pointed to. Both report how much they tighten and widen and the raw wins they take from the lost requests; both are scored in Section 9 and split by term in Section 10.
- **The scorecard by outcome term, and what crossing would have taken (Section 10 [2]).** Pair A at about the same win rate split by the request's logged outcome (won with a cover, won without, lost with a richer print, not winnable), each term with its own numbers and its contribution to the pooled delta, which must add up (asserted). Then the lost requests of the test months by decile of how far production was behind the print: the count, how far above our model mid the street paid, and what the street's winning trade was worth under the record, common and other-side exits; the same by band width, and a band width x distance heatmap of the other-side markout. The tightening a crossing rule needs is the distance; the value of getting there is the print's markout net of what is paid over it. A crossing rule sized from this table is the next step, not part of this version.
- **The cover in price (v1.0.2.1).** Every scorecard row carries the cover in price beside the bp: the margin over the cover on the cover wins in cents per 100 par (d x DV01), the cover saved on the wins both won in cents per 100 par, dollars per fill and thousands of dollars a month, and the price paid over the print on the new wins in the same three units. Section 11 prints a scoreboard of every setting against production as is on those columns; the monthly tables, the by-width cut and the crossing table carry the same.
- **Section 11** adds the two-sided settings in one table (new wins on lost requests, median tightening, how much richer than the print, the lost-term delta, the pooled delta) and the audits to the registry.

What moved in the machinery: DV01 is priced in Section 5 [2] (before the thresholds) rather than Section 6 [1]; `bt_price_to_yield` (a price2yield call on the cover) is gone; `requote_const` accepts a charge of either sign; the pricing service counts as down only when every call of the first full batch raised ('missing from response' is an input it cannot price). Caches: the data fingerprints change with the re-pull, so every stage rebuilds (about an hour); the factor model and quantile stages would reuse on an unchanged panel.

## What differs from the variant notebook

- **The exit of record** is the comparable-bond bucket exit (characteristics only), not the IPCA-peer exit of lab 14f. The peer exit needs the Section 11 baskets; the bucket was its circularity guard and the two sat within a bp of MAE against the own print. The sceptical set is therefore the bucket exit, the common markout and the own other-side print; the hedged valuation is not here (no request-level covariance). The realised WFTrade round trip is reported beside, on the fills the desk actually sold.
- **The risk axis** is the book's gross DV01 over the holding window, not the ex-ante sd under the lab's covariance.
- **The extended charge** rescales gamma on the extended population's own median width (the v1.3 run scaled it on the covered bonds and over-widened).
- **The test months** are the months whose calibration has at least `min_fit_rows` of production's wins with a band and a markout of record behind it; the first band month is calibration only. The variant notebook scored that month with a scale ratio of 1, so its `gamma = k / median W` was set on an uncalibrated median width (about 2.4 bp) and then applied to calibrated widths of 10 to 16 bp: its "k = 2" charge was 8 to 13 bp at the median, not 2. Here gamma is set on the first calibrated month, so k means what it says.
- **The pricing service** is read through the lab's cache first (`artifacts_ipca_lab/pricing_cache.parquet`, the same inputs), stops after one fully failed batch and says so, and the notebook refuses to score when the cover yields did not price: without them every win is scored against our own quote and any widening loses it.

## Running it

```
python3 tools/py2nb.py muni_width_charge_research.py muni_width_charge_research.ipynb   # the converter in the scratch tools (percent format -> ipynb)
```

Run from the lab root (`MUNI_LAB_ROOT`, default the current directory) with `data_pipeline/` holding `muni_data_pipeline.py` and the pulled stores, and `prod_pfills/` holding the pfill snapshots. Everything it writes goes to `artifacts_width_charge/` (cache, figures, the matrix parquet, the registry). The pricing service is called for cover yields and DV01 and cached on disk; a `PRICING_OVERRIDE` global with the same signature replaces it away from the desk's network. `MUNI_WC_CFG` takes JSON overrides of `RunConfig` fields (used by the smoke run).

Expected runtime on the desk's data (v1.0.1 ran in 53 minutes with the pricing service up): the factor model walk-forward and the quantile models are the slow stages the first time (the lab's figures suggest 15 to 25 minutes together); the replay, exits and frontier grid another 25 minutes; the 14 settings a few minutes each to quote and score. A rerun with nothing changed reuses every stage.
